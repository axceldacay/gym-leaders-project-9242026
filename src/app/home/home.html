Kanto Region

The Kanto region is located east of Johto, which together form a joint landmass that is south of Sinnoh. The majority of the Kanto region are forests and plains. Many of the major Kanto cities are in the center of the region, with close access to virtually every environment such as the sea, mountains, forests, etc. Kanto is mostly dominated by land, but has a large bay in the middle which opens to a sea in the southwest. Kanto has a contrast between urban cities and rural towns. Kanto has several mountain ranges, which are mostly in northern and western Kanto. Indigo Plateau and Victory Road mountain range divides Kanto from the neighboring Johto region.

At some point, Porygon was created by researchers in Kanto. At some point, Mr. Fuji discovered Mew deep in a jungle and coined its name. Later, with other scientists, he managed to gather some of Mew's DNA and used it in his mansion to create a modified clone of it, aiming it to be the most powerful Pokémon of all, which resulted in the birth of the Legendary Pokémon Mewtwo. Sometime after its creation however, Mewtwo escapes and destroys the mansion, hiding away in Cerulean Cave. At some point, Professor Rowan travels to Kanto to study Pokedex systems with Professor Oak.

Johto Region

The Johto region, long regarded as a land of tradition and reverence, preserves a history interwoven with the presence of legendary Pokémon and the customs of its people. The cultural and spiritual fabric of Johto rests upon sites of ancient significance, each testifying to the enduring relationship between humans and the divine guardians of the natural world. Foremost among these monuments are the twin towers once erected in Ecruteak City: the Bell Tower and the Brass Tower. The Bell Tower, still standing, remains the perch of Ho-Oh, the rainbow guardian said to bless the worthy with its radiance. Its sister tower, the Brass Tower, was lost to flame in a calamity whose cause has been long debated. Within that blaze, three Pokémon perished. Out of compassion, Ho-Oh granted them new life, and they arose as the legendary beasts—Raikou, Entei, and Suicune. Each embodies a natural force present in the disaster: the lightning that struck the tower, the fire that consumed it, and the rain that quenched the flames. To this day, the beasts roam freely, untethered by human control.

In counterbalance to Ho-Oh stands Lugia, the guardian of the seas. Dwelling deep within the Whirl Islands, Lugia remains hidden from civilization, suppressing its own immense power to preserve harmony between sea and sky. Its presence is both feared and revered, ensuring that balance is maintained within the region.

Beyond Ecruteak, the land bears other traces of ancient mystery. The Ruins of Alph house the enigmatic Unown, creatures whose forms resemble letters of an archaic script. Scholars speculate that these beings were once venerated, perhaps even consulted by early civilizations, though their true nature remains obscured. Within the sacred Ilex Forest lies a shrine dedicated to Celebi, the forest guardian. Said to traverse the flow of time itself, Celebi symbolizes renewal and the eternal bond between nature and humankind.

Taken together, these accounts illustrate the core philosophy of Johto’s heritage: destruction and rebirth, balance and restraint, reverence and humility. The region’s history does not simply recount events of the past, but rather serves as instruction for the present—reminding all who dwell in Johto that harmony with nature and respect for the sacred are the foundations upon which peace endures.
